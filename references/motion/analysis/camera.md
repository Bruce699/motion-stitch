# Camera, framing and projection

The strongest shared habit is **changing how much information the viewer can see, then giving that information a stable frame**. The films do not all rely on continuous camera movement. Some of the richest passages contain very little global movement: a lens crosses text, stitching forms on leather, or a wordmark is constructed inside a fixed page.

This audit visually inspected all five film overviews and **35 temporal windows at 6 fps**, usually four seconds each: 30 newly extracted windows and five reused transition windows. It also compares seven original sequence pairs. The sample is deliberately varied, not random or exhaustive; it does not establish a percentage of footage with a moving camera. [The searchable records](camera.json) contain every interval, semantic scene ID, framing, direction, projection qualification and evidence path.

All times below refer to the selected local reels. Our editorial splices are excluded from original sequence-pair conclusions. One evidence sheet includes the Liquid Glass splice at 34.8348 in its last frame; that observation explicitly ends 34.8. Film sound was not used to infer musical synchronization.

## The recurring approaches

These counts deduplicate semantic scenes, not sampled frames or duplicate specialist reports. They rank **film coverage first**, then distinct scenes cited by those observations. A scene containing two useful views can support more than one approach. Counts describe this selected sample, not how much screen time an approach occupies. Five films represent three companies because three films are Apple.

| Approach | Films | Distinct semantic scenes | What is actually recurring |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Frontal reading plane | 5 | 22 | A screen, diagram or graphic is presented sufficiently front-on to read without following a large orientation change. This can be a stage within a longer move. |
| Scale reveals context | 5 | 17 | One detail becomes part of a larger interface, family or layout. This includes graphic scaling; it is not a claim of physical dolly movement. |
| Local action inside a stable frame | 5 | 15 | The lens, UI, threads, dots or glyphs supply the important movement while the broader frame stays still or comparatively quiet. |
| Deliberate detail framing | 5 | 11 | The frame excludes context to make one construction, material or alignment inspectable. |
| Move, then hold or nearly settle | 5 | 8 | A scale/orientation change reaches a readable state instead of maintaining equal travel throughout. These are visual observations, not fitted camera easing curves. |
| Preserve a visual anchor through change | 4 | 19 | The device, central icon, letter or page remains an orientation reference as content changes. |
| Continuous spatial handoff | 4 | 10 | A shared interface or motif carries attention into another content chapter without an unrelated new view. |
| Restrained view drift | 4 | 6 | Small relative translation or orientation change accompanies a material/product view. |
| Change viewpoint with an ordinary cut | 3 | 8 | Macro, full object and oblique perspectives can be selected directly instead of connected by a long flight. |
| Relative rotation | 3 | 7 | Visible faces/orientations change. This is consistent with an object turntable or camera orbit; the original rig is unknown. |
| Contrast flat and angled presentation | 3 | 4 | A readable product/application view alternates with a more dimensional inspection view. |
| Oblique depth demonstration | 2 | 5 | An angled view makes detached layers, thickness or occlusion visible. |
| Lateral survey | 2 | 4 | A row or larger interface moves through a relatively consistent inspection scale. |

These are options, not rules. The narrower distribution of the last two approaches does not make them weaker; their usefulness depends on the subject.

## Each film's camera grammar

### Liquid Glass: inspect the material, then recover the interface

The close studies at **6–9.8 seconds** treat a numeral rim like an object with volume. Little relative drift is enough because refraction, highlights and overlapping edges already contain information. A wider clock view is introduced by a cut rather than by explaining every millimeter of travel. [CAM-01 evidence](../local/analysis/camera/apple-liquid-glass-006.jpg)

At **11–18 seconds**, oversized Photos/Safari pieces resolve into an icon, Dock and complete phone. Relative orientation levels toward a readable frontal view. The compass needle is also moving independently: describing the whole event as a camera orbit would miss the assembly itself. The rapid reduction ends in a much quieter full-phone presentation. [CAM-35](../local/analysis/transitions/apple-liquid-glass-011.jpg), [CAM-02](../local/analysis/camera/apple-liquid-glass-014.jpg)

The Notes demonstration at **79–83 seconds** takes the opposite route: keep a restrained, low oblique plane while controls regroup above it. The tilt makes separation visible; there is no need to continually orbit around the task. [CAM-04](../local/analysis/camera/apple-liquid-glass-079.jpg)

**Useful choices:** macro material framing; slow relative drift; angled layer explanations; a large change of scale to restore context; small holds in frontal product views. **Counterexample to constant movement:** the magnifier initially moves across a stable paragraph at 31–32 seconds, before the broader framing enlarges. [CAM-03](../local/analysis/camera/apple-liquid-glass-031.jpg)

### iOS 7: the device is a stable reference, and depth explains layers

The hardware supplies an external coordinate frame. At **100–104 seconds**, the framing settles before Notification Center descends inside the phone. At **112–116**, Notifications retracts, home appears, and Messages opens while the outer device retains its position and scale. The app's scale animation is not a whole-device camera zoom. [CAM-09](../local/analysis/camera/apple-ios-7-100.jpg), [CAM-31](../local/analysis/transitions/apple-ios-7-112.jpg)

At **109–113**, the exploded notification/icon/wallpaper planes converge while the relative orientation becomes more frontal. The initial angle explains depth; the ending resembles normal use. This is a strong example of an orientation change serving an explanation. It does not establish whether a camera or the entire phone rig rotated. [CAM-10](../local/analysis/camera/apple-ios-7-109.jpg)

The closing beauty reprise uses cuts between a thin, side-biased product crop and a more frontal icon close-up (**229–233**). There is no requirement to make those angles part of one continuous orbit. [CAM-12](../local/analysis/camera/apple-ios-7-229.jpg)

**Useful choices:** anchored bezel; gentle relative product rotation; exploded-layer angle returning to front; interface action inside a settled device; direct cuts to important detail. **Counterexample to continuous drift:** the folder demonstration at 144–148 keeps the phone comparatively quiet while the home-screen elements change. [CAM-11](../local/analysis/camera/apple-ios-7-144.jpg)

### Yosemite: a guided survey that progressively widens

The Dock sequence at **22–26 and 31–35 seconds** behaves like a guided lateral inspection. Icons travel left through the frame at a comparable scale while individual icons change locally. A slight orientation bias gives the shelf volume without making the information hard to locate. This could be achieved by moving a view or translating the composition; the perceptual result is the finding. [CAM-14](../local/analysis/camera/apple-yosemite-022.jpg), [CAM-15](../local/analysis/camera/apple-yosemite-031.jpg)

At **36–40**, a sidebar grows into a recognizable Finder window. At **50–57**, widgets give way to the desktop and then the complete laptop. The audience is repeatedly shown that the detail belongs to something larger. The final retreat slows before the centered signature. [CAM-16](../local/analysis/camera/apple-yosemite-036.jpg), [CAM-17](../local/analysis/camera/apple-yosemite-050.jpg), [CAM-18](../local/analysis/camera/apple-yosemite-054.jpg)

**Useful choices:** horizontal survey; consistent inspection scale; scale changes attached to a recognizable piece of UI; widening from component to system to device. **Counterexample to treating all backgrounds as spatial depth:** the sidebar's photographic backgrounds change as examples of translucency; they are not evidence of camera parallax.

### Airbnb: alternate precise flat diagrams with tactile object views

At **17–21 seconds**, the view jumps from spacing to a corner curve and then rapidly retreats to reveal the button inside its form. At **19–23**, the frame travels from that form to the header arrow, which becomes the next icon-construction specimen. These moves have specific informational destinations. [CAM-19](../local/analysis/camera/airbnb-design-system-017.jpg), [CAM-33](../local/analysis/transitions/airbnb-design-system-019.jpg)

The suitcase at **27–31** uses a centered assembly shot, a nearly held stitching insert, and a cut back to the finished icon. The later gem/paw/bed succession preserves central product staging while allowing relative turns and different materials. A cut, rather than a flight, is enough to compare them. [CAM-20](../local/analysis/camera/airbnb-design-system-027.jpg), [CAM-21](../local/analysis/camera/airbnb-design-system-031.jpg)

At **53–57**, a frontal wall of cards gives way to angled floating sample plates, then a quiet mark. Flatness and dimensionality are contrasting modes, not a single projection applied everywhere. [CAM-24](../local/analysis/camera/airbnb-design-system-053.jpg)

**Useful choices:** front-on specification views; macro inserts; centered product turntable-like behavior; purposeful scale reversals; flat-to-dimensional contrast. **Counterexample to an always-moving camera:** the palette and stitching both permit holds while information changes. [Palette evidence](../local/analysis/camera/airbnb-design-system-038.jpg)

### OpenAI: the page is the stage

The marginal page labels repeatedly remain stationary while the main subject changes size, shape, density or position. At **6–10, 19–23, 48–52, 59–63, 73–77 and 93–97**, this is visible evidence of graphic choreography inside an editorial frame. Calling those changes a dolly, orbit or depth pass would introduce information the pixels do not provide.

The circle can shrink into the O of a wordmark (**48–52**); the Blossom can enlarge for contour inspection and hold (**59–63**); dots can spread and gather (**73–77**). A rich range of attention changes is possible without a conspicuous three-dimensional camera. [CAM-27](../local/analysis/camera/openai-brand-2025-048.jpg), [CAM-28](../local/analysis/camera/openai-brand-2025-059.jpg), [CAM-29](../local/analysis/camera/openai-brand-2025-073.jpg)

**Useful choices:** fixed page furniture; independent local scale; contour/detail inserts; stable center for motif changes; layout changes instead of spatial flights. **Limit:** the film also includes a photographic chapter; the close camera sample here concerns its designed graphic passages, not a claim that every photographic frame uses the same setup.

### With the elements, against them, or independent of them?

Three relationships are visible:

1. **Independent local motion in a quiet view.** The phone is steady while its panel slides; the paragraph initially stays steady while its lens travels; the page stays steady while dots disperse. This gives the action a reliable frame of reference.
2. **Global framing and assembly happening together.** Photos petals descend while the base's relative orientation changes; Yosemite widens while windows return to the desktop; Airbnb broadens while cards accumulate. The two motion layers explain different scales of the system.
3. **Reframing that deliberately changes the destination.** Airbnb retreats from a corner to the form, then advances toward its arrow. Yosemite's lateral menu survey switches into the O enlargement, then returns to a lateral Dock survey. The change of direction is attached to a visible target.

The sampled scenes do **not** justify a universal rule that camera movement should oppose object movement, nor a reliable count of countertracking shots. Screen-space movement alone cannot determine the world-space relationship. A card held at center while its surroundings change might result from a tracking camera, a moving parent rig, or composited transforms.

## Perspective, orthographic and isometric: what the evidence permits

- **Frontal is a viewing direction, not a projection setting.** A front-on screen can look nearly identical under perspective, orthographic projection or a 2D composition.
- **Oblique does not mean isometric.** The Notes sheet, Airbnb plates and bed icon expose multiple surfaces, but this sample does not establish the equal axis foreshortening and projection conditions needed to call them mathematically isometric.
- **Foreshortening alone does not prove perspective.** Tilted rectangles become compressed under orthographic projection too. Thickness, shadows and occlusion establish depicted volume or layering, not a particular lens.
- **Perspective-looking passages do exist.** The Liquid Glass petal close-up places much larger near petals across smaller distant petals and exposes varying faces. The laptop body has a familiar receding-plane treatment. These support a dimensional perspective-like reading; they do not recover the original render camera or focal length.
- **The clearest confirmed distinction is behavioral:** flat/front-facing graphic construction versus changing dimensional orientation and depth. Preserving that distinction is more useful here than inventing camera settings.

## Ways to apply this vocabulary

Treat these as authored choices to test against a scene's purpose:

1. Start by deciding what information the next framing reveals: material, construction, interaction or system context.
2. Use close framing when a detail would otherwise be too small to inspect.
3. Let the detail return to a recognizable interface or family when context matters.
4. A quiet frame is a valid cinematic choice; make the important object move instead.
5. Keep the bezel, page or a persistent motif as a reference when several interface states change.
6. Use an oblique view to reveal layer order; return toward front if the next beat depends on reading.
7. Let object assembly and global framing do different jobs instead of attributing everything to a camera flight.
8. A lateral survey can compare siblings without repeatedly changing inspection scale.
9. A close insert followed by a cut back to the whole object can be clearer than continuous travel.
10. Choose a destination for a push-in-like move; the Airbnb back arrow is a particularly explicit example.
11. A deliberate reversal of scale can be coherent when the same object bridges it.
12. Preserve an anchor across a handoff when visual continuity matters.
13. Alternatively, contrast flat and angled presentation when moving from application to material study.
14. Allow a move to settle before a reading beat or closing mark.
15. Use depth and angle as explanatory devices, not default ornament on every UI screen.
16. Distinguish inner app zooms from motion of the whole device so timing can be controlled separately.
17. Do not require camera continuity across every cut; several strong comparisons use direct viewpoint changes.
18. When reproducing a reference, match the visible framing trajectory first; leave the original hidden rig as unknown.

These recommendations are interpretations drawn from the observations, not statements of the original teams' intentions. The options include both continuous movement and stillness, both dimensional scenes and flat graphic stages.
