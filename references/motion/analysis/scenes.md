# Semantic scene audit

This map treats a scene as one coherent information beat, even when it contains several camera views, changes of scale, or cuts. The strongest shared pattern is **show the part, explain a property, then show what it belongs to**. The films often build a visual argument rather than merely cycle through attractive screenshots.

## Evidence and limits

All five overviews and all 34 chronological contact sheets were visually inspected. Six additional sequences were extracted from the actual local videos at six frames per second: Airbnb suitcase construction, iOS 7 Photos construction, Liquid Glass Photos construction, Liquid Glass Notes toolbar, Yosemite glyph transformations, and OpenAI Blossom construction. This report uses those pixels, not OCR or the preexisting section descriptions alone. The six finer sheets are under `local/analysis/scenes/`; their labels are nominal seek/sample times, not measured easing curves.

The 69 entries cover each selected reel continuously. Boundaries are approximately accurate to one second, except our splice locations taken from edit lists. A boundary marks a semantic handoff; it need not coincide with a cut. Entries can include a short transition into the next subject. `scenes.json` provides source spans and internal splices. No claim here establishes authoring software, actual physics, real-time rendering, or precise interpolation. Motion vocabulary describes visible state changes.

Our Liquid Glass, iOS 7 and Airbnb reels are recuts. The original editing cannot be inferred across those joins. In particular, the Liquid Glass feature recap LG-15 contains one internal join introduced by us and also starts at our splice; it is a useful category of content but not evidence of the original film's pacing. Yosemite and OpenAI are complete films. Coverage counts below concern retained material, not complete-film prevalence.

## Scene taxonomy

1. **Material introduction.** Delay full product recognition while showing edge, thickness, sheen, texture or optical behavior. Liquid Glass LG-01/LG-05; Airbnb AB-05/AB-06. The promise is tactile before it is functional.
2. **Construction study.** Reveal grids, outlines, handles or component pieces, then the finished mark/UI. I7-07/I7-08; YO-03; AB-01/AB-03/AB-04; OA-03/OA-06/OA-07. It makes the design feel reasoned and deliberately made.
3. **Controlled comparison.** Preserve framing or a shared shape while changing one principal variable: weight, color, state, surface, weather or wallpaper. I7-11/I7-15; AB-04/AB-08; OA-02/OA-03/OA-08. The viewer does not have to relearn the composition to notice the difference.
4. **Family survey.** Present siblings one at a time, side by side, or in a common place. It can contain many cuts while remaining one idea: a shared design language. YO-04 is one Dock survey, not fourteen separate scenes; LG-03 is one cross-device argument.
5. **Layer explanation.** Separate normally coincident surfaces so their relationship becomes visible, then restore a usable composition. I7-09 is the clearest exploded view. LG-09 gives a toolbar its own plane without losing the document.
6. **Functional microstory.** Show a starting state, an action/change, and a readable result or return: drawers, switching apps, a filter, a compacting toolbar. It need not contain a hand or cursor. I7-05/I7-16/I7-17; LG-09/LG-11.
7. **Context demonstration.** Keep the design recognizably embedded in the screen, card, page or device where it will be used. Construction studies are repeatedly paid off with a real interface rather than left as detached abstract art.
8. **Detail-to-whole reveal.** Change the scale of explanation: icon to home screen, window to desktop, typography to menu bar, component to listing. This is an information strategy; it does not imply that every instance uses a literal camera zoom.
9. **Accumulation.** Begin with one legible object and add a family around it. AB-10, OA-11 and LG-14 show breadth after providing an anchor. Dense compositions are often conclusions, not starting points.
10. **Atmospheric/editorial chapter.** Let content or an expressive arrangement carry the scene: I7-11 Weather; OA-05 typography; OA-09 photography. These are valid departures from construction demonstrations.
11. **Reprise and signature.** Return to already understood forms, then reduce complexity and finish on a recognizable mark. LG-16, I7-20/I7-21, AB-11, OA-11/OA-12. Repetition closes the argument instead of introducing another capability.

## Within-scene choreography principles

- **Give the beat a concrete question.** “How does this surface adapt?”, “How is this icon built?”, or “How many siblings share this rule?” is more useful than choosing a visual effect first. This is an interpretation of the scene organization, not a claim about the creators' intent.
- **Keep an identity through changes of representation.** Airbnb's suitcase remains identifiable through outline, wrapping pieces, finished object, stitching close-up and stickers. OpenAI keeps the circle legible as dot, letter construction, palette swatch and image aperture. Continuity of subject allows bolder changes of scale or representation.
- **Change the explanatory variable while holding enough else steady.** Three iOS 7 phones undergo matching screen states over different wallpapers (172–183s). OpenAI compares outline and filled glyphs in stable positions (24–28s). These are demonstrations, not arbitrary variation.
- **Construct in a perceptible order.** iOS 7 Photos petals accumulate around a center (97.7–98.7s); Liquid Glass petals descend from separated depth to the base (119.5–121.5s); Airbnb suitcase strips cover a recognizable outline (26.5–28s). Stagger is useful because the parts remain interpretable, not because everything must cascade.
- **Show both the transformation and its finished state.** Yosemite's line glyphs reconfigure, then briefly become clean symbols; OpenAI removes construction handles for the finished Blossom; Music navigation returns to an expanded state. A result needs enough compositional stability to be recognizable. This does not require all motion to stop.
- **Use a known carrier when the internal behavior is the point.** The same phone, paragraph, page or icon outline gives changing material and state a stable reference. LG-04, LG-09, I7-15, AB-04 and OA-03 are especially clear examples.
- **Let scale change the level of explanation.** Stitching deserves a macro shot, but the suitcase must also be seen whole. Letter control points deserve enlargement, but the wordmark must also read at a usable scale. Detail alone proves craft; the return to context proves relevance.
- **Make relationships visible before adding complexity.** The notification/icons/wallpaper separation in I7-09 is explanatory, while the later switcher shows a practical interaction. LG-09 makes toolbar depth visible before its menu variations. Avoid confusing a layer explanation with a decorative pile of panels.
- **Reuse a rule across siblings to establish a system.** Common grids, framing, scale, background, or choreography make variants comparable. Different objects need not have identical shape or material; Airbnb's leather, crystal-like and furry icons deliberately differ while sharing their presentation role.
- **Build density from an anchor.** AB-10 begins with a readable feature card before its larger collection. OA-11 adds covers and posters progressively. The effect is abundance without making the viewer identify every object at the same instant.
- **Use context as an active demonstrator.** Translucent surfaces need a changing or contrasting image beneath them. LG-07/LG-09/LG-11, I7-12/I7-15 and YO-05 demonstrate the relation; AB-08 tests surface tokens against ocean footage. Background imagery is doing explanatory work.
- **A family montage can be a single semantic scene.** Utility apps in iOS 7 and glyphs in Yosemite demonstrate a shared vocabulary. Splitting these at every cut would falsely inflate repetitions and hide the purpose of the sequence.
- **Repetition can change purpose.** The Photos icon first establishes appearance, later construction; the phone home screen first introduces the system, later summarizes it. A repeated asset is not automatically redundant when the explanatory role changes.
- **A brief neutral ending gives the sequence a destination.** Brand marks on sparse fields close every selected reel. This is the most literal shared closing convention, but should not be ranked as a rich choreography idea merely because it appears five times.

## Exceptions and differences worth retaining

- iOS 7 is often patient and demonstrative. A static phone can carry a useful interaction; continuous external movement is not necessary. Its 0–43s hardware prologue is categorically different from the subsequent interface studies.
- Liquid Glass mixes spectacle and explanation. Its materials use depth and optical distortion, while the Notes example is a relatively stable carrier for changing control states. Do not generalize the macro opening into a requirement that all UI become a sculpture.
- Yosemite compresses a system tour into roughly a minute. Its Dock is an extended family survey; the many icons should not count as many independent endorsements of one principle.
- Airbnb moves quickly among typography, controls, icons, tokens and actual components. The suitcase is an unusually elaborate miniature story within that fast rhythm. Its material sequence is not representative of every beat.
- OpenAI is primarily a flat graphic/editorial film. Its major depth is conceptual—primitive to identity to application—not necessarily a spatial camera move. Typography can overlap, crop aggressively and briefly become unreadable; full readability is not a universal rule for expressive type passages.
- Photography is preserved where it is the content or demonstrates a surface. OpenAI includes full photographic scenes. Calling this collection exclusively UI-only would be incorrect.
- No scene map supplies universal easing constants, fixed durations, or a mandate to use 3D, bezels, spring motion or morphs. The defensible commonality is explanatory organization, not a single house style.
