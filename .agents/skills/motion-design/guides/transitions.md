# Transitions

Use this guide for a handoff between content scenes or a purposeful internal shot change. Inspect both sides before editing the boundary.

Identify what changes and what, if anything, survives: identity, geometry, color, position, direction, framing or a shared field. Decide whether continuity, comparison, surprise or a reset best serves this handoff.

## Available approaches

| Approach | Use when | Decisions and failure checks |
|---|---|---|
| Direct cut | The new idea reads immediately, or a sharp change is desired | Check where attention lands. Do not assume the absence of a morph means the transition is unfinished. |
| Graphic match | Different subjects share a salient property | Match what remains noticeable at speed. A similar shape alone may be too weak if position and scale both jump. |
| Persistent element | One recognizable subject can connect two compositions | Decide what survives and what changes around it. Do not force identity onto unrelated content. |
| Directional replacement | Travel can pass attention from exit to entrance | Inspect the pair's direction and speed. Avoid a barely visible reversal caused by conflicting moves. |
| Overlapping arrival and exit | Coexistence explains assembly, containment or a relationship | Give attention a clear owner during overlap. Watch accidental clutter or ambiguous occlusion. |
| Scale or aperture bridge | A meaningful shape/detail can open onto another context | Establish the target before enlargement or masking. Avoid disguising arbitrary content swaps as explanation. |
| Shared background or frame | Foreground subjects change while the presentation remains coherent | Preserve the useful constant. Color/field continuity can support a plain cut without requiring object continuity. |
| Density or chapter reset | Attention needs a new starting point | Make the reset deliberate. Sparse does not have to mean slow, and a recurring marker need not appear at every boundary. |

An incoming scene can be anticipated by motion, a glimpse, changed scale or a visible target. Use anticipation when it creates expectation worth fulfilling; not every cut requires a buildup.

## Review the pair

Play enough of the outgoing scene to establish its motion, through the incoming scene's first meaningful state. Check the attention path, recognition time, accidental flashes and whether the handoff consumes more time than the ideas it connects.

Then watch the larger sequence. Repeated elaborate handoffs can weaken contrast; repeated cuts can be an intentional rhythm. Choose based on the piece, not a quota of transition types.

When studying an edited reference reel, distinguish original cuts from joins introduced by the person collecting it. A manufactured join is not evidence of the original transition design.
