# Scene structure

Use this guide when deciding what a sequence is about, how much information appears at once, or where it should end. A scene can be explanatory or expressive; it does not have to demonstrate a task.

For each content scene, name its subject and intended change in understanding or feeling. Group shots by that purpose. A cut to another angle of the same object need not begin a new content scene.

## Detail and context

- **Use when:** a small property deserves inspection, or the viewer needs to understand where it belongs.
- **Decide:** which detail matters; when context is needed; whether the order is whole-to-detail, detail-to-whole, or a comparison between them.
- **Options:** a crop, cut, scale change, mask or camera move can connect the views.
- **Failure:** the close-up is attractive but anonymous, or the reveal ends before the relationship becomes recognizable.
- **Check:** can the viewer connect the important detail to its broader context at the intended beat?

## Construction and result

- **Use when:** craft, structure or assembly is part of the message.
- **Decide:** which construction information actually explains the form; whether to build toward the finished state or expose the structure beneath it.
- **Options:** guides, contours, parts, layers, material changes or progressive assembly.
- **Failure:** decorative technical marks imply precision without explaining anything.
- **Check:** is the connection between construction and finished form visible? See [visual treatment](visual-treatment.md) for guides and measurements.

## Compare a family

- **Use when:** variation, adaptability or range is the subject.
- **Decide:** what remains consistent enough to compare; which property varies; whether comparison is sequential or simultaneous.
- **Options:** shared framing, matched scale, repeated geometry or a stable interface carrying different states.
- **Failure:** so many properties change that the intended difference becomes hard to identify.
- **Check:** can the relevant difference be noticed at playback speed?

## Demonstrate a change

- **Use when:** an interaction or transformation supplies the story.
- **Decide:** the initial state, visible cause or implied action, and meaningful result. A cursor or hand is optional.
- **Failure:** motion happens, but the result or causal order is unclear.
- **Check:** does the action read from the actual content without explanatory overlay copy?

## Expand or concentrate

- **Use when:** moving between one specimen and a broader collection creates scale, energy or closure.
- **Decide:** which first object establishes the presentation; how density changes; whether the ending communicates breadth or asks for detailed reading.
- **Failure:** every item demands attention, or an arbitrary collage substitutes for a coherent collection.
- **Check:** is there an intentional attention hierarchy even when individual items are no longer readable?

These structures can be combined, reversed or omitted. Do not automatically give every scene a construction sequence, an explosion and a device reveal. An expressive scene can instead sustain a motif, rhythm or material experience. Review its contribution to the entire piece, including contrast with its neighbors.
