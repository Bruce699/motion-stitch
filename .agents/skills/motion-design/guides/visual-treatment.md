# Visual treatment

Use this guide for the presentation of UI, graphic objects, surfaces and their surroundings. Start with approved artwork and the actual interface. Preserve source fidelity unless transformation or abstraction is requested.

## Choose the amount of context

- **Hardware:** useful when device identity, physical scale or use context matters. It may be cropped rather than shown completely.
- **Screen-shaped plane or window:** retains layout proportions without requiring modeled hardware.
- **Isolated component:** makes geometry, behavior or material easier to inspect.
- **Diagram or macro crop:** exposes a relationship or detail that normal presentation would hide.

Switch levels when the scene's purpose changes. Do not require a bezel around every interface or make every component a floating card.

## Construction guides and measurement

Also retrieved as **rulers**, **grids**, **wireframes**, **spacing guides**, **contour drawing**, or **technical overlays**.

- **Use when:** alignment, spacing, curvature, proportion or assembly is worth explaining, or a technical visual language is requested.
- **Decide:** which geometry the marks refer to; when they appear; what relationship they reveal; when they recede.
- **Options:** baselines, bounds, dimensions, tangents, control points, shared axes, outlines or progressive contour traces.
- **Failure:** arbitrary lines and numbers imply a real specification without measuring anything, or overwhelm the actual form.
- **Check:** derive factual dimensions from source geometry and label units consistently. Keep illustrative diagrams free of false precision. If explanatory construction is claimed, make its relationship to the finished object visible.

## Give the background a job

A neutral stage can isolate a specimen. Content behind a surface can reveal tint or transparency. A saturated field can distinguish a chapter. Photography can supply content, atmosphere or an editorial composition.

Distinguish the field outside an interface from content inside or behind it. Choose contrast and density for the important subject; a quiet background need not be white.

## Choose separation cues

Use silhouette, negative space, edge contrast, outlines, occlusion, shadows, depth or optical material behavior as appropriate. White-on-white may need a subtle edge or shadow; flat black geometry may need neither. Shadow strength should fit the implied distance and lighting rather than a universal polish setting.

Material richness can vary by subject. A textured object and a precise flat control can belong to the same composition if their roles are clear. Typography may function as readable content, a construction specimen or an expressive shape; choose the role for the current beat.

## Focus and material inspection

Selective softness, changing angle, highlights and close framing can reveal particular properties. Keep the critical feature clear at the intended beat. Check that blur supports attention rather than concealing poor separation or low source resolution. Verify the final output; preview softness does not establish a particular optical implementation.

None of glass, shadows, hardware, photorealism or depth is a universal requirement. Select a coherent treatment for the actual content and requested feeling.
